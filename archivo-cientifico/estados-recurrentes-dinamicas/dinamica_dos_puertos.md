The carrier consists of nine labelled active sites 0,...,8 in row-major order on a wired 3×3 square, one sink, and firing threshold four. Stable heights are 0,1,2,3. The column-emitter Laplacian has negative entry D[v,u] for an arc from u to v; each column sum gives that source's sink-edge multiplicity. The elementary exchange used below changes D[v,u] by −1 and D[u,v] by +1, keeping diagonal thresholds fixed. The strict and relaxed families have different arc budgets, explicitly stated below.

## 1. The seven-site core and the two corner ports

Choose opposite ports `0` and `8`, and the core `W=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7)`. Remove all edges between the ports and the core, treating the corresponding missing channels as sink edges, and insert two directed internal exchange valves `2→1` and `6→7`. The common core's exact reduced toppling matrix in vertex order `W` is

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
4&-2&0&-1&0&0&0\\
0&4&0&0&-1&0&0\\
0&0&4&-1&0&-1&0\\
-1&0&-1&4&-1&0&-1\\
0&-1&0&-1&4&0&0\\
0&0&-1&0&0&4&0\\
0&0&0&-1&0&-2&4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This core is dissipative, strongly connected, and **non-symmetric**. Its outgoing sink-budget vector is `(3,1,2,0,2,1,3)` and exact arithmetic gives

\[
\det A=10440,\qquad K(A)=\mathbb Z^7/A\mathbb Z^7
\cong\mathbb Z_3\oplus\mathbb Z_{3480}.
\]

Two original-labelled coupling columns at the ports will be written in the core's order:

\[
a_s=(2,0,2,0,0,0,0)^T,\qquad b_s=(0,0,0,0,2,0,2)^T,
\]

for the **strict** construction, and

\[
a_r=(1,0,1,0,0,0,0)^T,\qquad b_r=(0,0,0,0,1,0,1)^T
\]

for the **relaxed** construction. All these transfers satisfy the sink-budget inequalities. The port thresholds remain four.

### Exactly which structures remain inside the specified exchange-valve rules?

In the **strict network**, both ports are isolated by exactly four eligible exchange valves. The complete outward valve set is

`(0→1), (0→3), (8→5), (8→7), (2→1), (6→7)`.

The strict inward version reverses **only the first four**: `(1→0), (3→0), (5→8), (7→8)`, with `(2→1), (6→7)` unchanged. Both are literal valid extensions of the edge-exchange operation defined at the start of this report, now using six distinct valves. Each network has **24 internal directed arcs and 12 sink arcs**.

In the **relaxed network**, the port-to-core channel multiplicities are reduced from two to one per neighboring core site; unused outgoing channels go to the sink. The inward reversal similarly shifts one channel per coupling from the core to the sink. Both relaxed networks have **20 internal arcs and 16 sink arcs**, preserving firing thresholds, dissipativity, and the seven-site core. **These networks are NOT generated by the specified edge-exchange valve rule**, and all results using them are marked as an explicit new carrier extension. This distinction is essential to interpreting the transitivity result.

We denote the four networks by `SP` (strict outward), `SQ` (strict inward), `RP` (relaxed outward), `RQ` (relaxed inward). Their complete original-labelled 9×9 matrices are stored in `casos.txt` and `certificado_reproducido.json`.

## 2. The two-port theorem (standard group principles, local application)

Let `A` be a finite dissipative core with critical group `K(A)` and two nonnegative integer coupling columns `a,b`, and let the two port thresholds be positive integers `d_0,d_1`. Assume there are no arcs between the ports, the outgoing sink budgets are nonnegative, and every active site can reach the sink. Reorder the sites as port 0, port 1, core to display the two extremes

\[
L_{\mathrm{out}}=\begin{pmatrix}
d_0&0&0\\0&d_1&0\\-a&-b&A
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L_{\mathrm{in}}=\begin{pmatrix}
d_0&0&-a^T\\0&d_1&-b^T\\0&0&A
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The ports are one-vertex SCCs and `A` represents the remaining SCCs. The **classical Speer SCC decomposition** implies literal labelled equality

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Rec}(L_{\mathrm{out}})
=\operatorname{Rec}(L_{\mathrm{in}})
=\{0,\ldots,d_0-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,d_1-1\}
\times\operatorname{Rec}(A).}
\]

Both networks have determinant and recurrent cardinality `d_0 d_1 det(A)`.

For a recurrent state `(k,l,x)`, a marked addition at port 0 in the outward network increments `k` modulo `d_0`, and on wrap-around adds the core vector `a` and stabilizes. Port 1 similarly increments `l` modulo `d_1`, adding `b` to the core on wrap-around. Thus these are **commuting skew-product permutations**. In the inward network their marked addition permutations are simply rotations of the two port digits, with no change in the core.

Define the subgroup of the core critical group and its index

\[
H=\langle[a],[b]\rangle\le K(A),\qquad
I=[K(A):H]=[\mathbb Z^m:A\mathbb Z^m+\mathbb Za+\mathbb Zb].
\]

The index `I` is exactly the product of the nonzero Smith diagonal entries of the **rectangular integer matrix** `[A | a | b]`, a finite arithmetic certificate requiring no recurrent-state enumeration. If `r_a` and `r_b` are the orders of `[a]` and `[b]` (denominators of `A^{-1}a` and `A^{-1}b`), then:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{ord}(T_0^{\mathrm{out}})=d_0r_a,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}(T_1^{\mathrm{out}})=d_1r_b,\\
&|\langle T_0^{\mathrm{out}},T_1^{\mathrm{out}}\rangle|
=d_0d_1|H|,\\
&\#\text{ joint outward orbits}=I,\\
&|\langle T_0^{\mathrm{in}},T_1^{\mathrm{in}}\rangle|
=d_0d_1,\qquad
\#\text{ joint inward orbits}=\det A.
\end{aligned}
\]

**Proof of the subgroup claim.** Projection onto the two port heights maps the marked outward action group onto `Z/d_0 × Z/d_1`. Its kernel consists precisely of complete port wrap-around actions, represented in the core by `[a]` and `[b]`, and therefore is `H`. The critical group acts freely and transitively on recurrent configurations. Consequently each subgroup orbit has cardinality equal to the order of that subgroup, and the number of orbits is its index. The stated group cardinalities and orbit indices follow. Each individual period uses the corresponding order of its core coupling class. The inward case is direct port rotation. All uses of SCC recurrence and the sandpile group action are standard mathematics; their instantiation in the particular graphs is the result certified here.

## 3. Four networks with the identical recurrent set

The four networks above share the same SCC diagonal blocks: `[4]`, `[4]`, and `A`. It follows *structurally*, without enumerating states, that

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{Rec}(SP)=\mathrm{Rec}(SQ)
=\mathrm{Rec}(RP)=\mathrm{Rec}(RQ)
=\{0,1,2,3\}_0\times\{0,1,2,3\}_8
\times\mathrm{Rec}(A).}
\]

Every set has `16 × 10,440 = 167,040` stable recurrent configurations. This equality concerns fixed original vertex labels, not merely cardinality, symmetry, or abstract isomorphism.

Yet **the abstract critical groups are not all isomorphic**:

| Graph | Smith factors >1 | Abstract group | Group order |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SP | `(2,12,6960)` | `Z2 ⊕ Z12 ⊕ Z6960` | 167,040 |
| SQ | `(2,12,6960)` | `Z2 ⊕ Z12 ⊕ Z6960` | 167,040 |
| RP | `(12,13920)` | `Z12 ⊕ Z13920` | 167,040 |
| RQ | `(12,13920)` | `Z12 ⊕ Z13920` | 167,040 |

The strict group needs **three** generators; the relaxed group needs **two**. The distinction is independently visible in the mod-2 matrix nullities (`3` for strict, `2` for relaxed). Consequently, an identical labelled recurrent configuration set **does not even determine the isomorphism class of the critical group**. The group operation is supplied by the toppling dynamics, not the mere inventory of recurrent states. The contribution here is the exact certification of these finite constructions using classical sandpile theory.

The exact subgroup indices can also be calculated *directly in the full 9-site group*, using Smith form of `[L | e_0 | e_8]`. The resulting indices are `2`, `10440`, `1`, `10440` for `SP,SQ,RP,RQ`, in agreement with the core-factorization theorem.

**Minimality in RP.** Because `K(RP) ≅ Z12 ⊕ Z13920` is noncyclic, no one group generator can act transitively on its 167,040 recurrent configurations. The two **particular** marked additions at corners 0 and 8 generate the full group (joint index one), so **exactly two marked generators are sufficient and necessary** for transitive reachability. Neither operator individually has this property. This establishes minimal generation by the two specified marked ports in the relaxed outward network.

## 5. Parity obstruction inside the *strict* exchange-valve subfamily

Retain the two outward or inward exchange-valve corners and permit any subset of the four further compatible internal exchange valves `2→1`, `2→5`, `6→3`, `6→7`. This gives **16** possible seven-site cores (including no internal valve). They were each rebuilt and checked for legal sink budgets in both port orientations. Every one has **even determinant**.

The strict corner couplings `a_s,b_s` have only even coordinates. Hence their images lie in `2K(A)`. Since `det(A)` is even, `K(A)/2K(A)` is nontrivial, proving that the two strict marked corner additions **cannot** act transitively across the entire recurrent set for any of these 16 variants. This is a **proven family-restricted obstruction**, not a universal impossibility for two ports.

For the certified two-internal-valve core, `K(A) ≅ Z3 ⊕ Z3480` and the strict couplings generate exactly `2K(A)`, of index two. Switching to the relaxed *odd* coupling columns breaks that obstruction: the rectangular Smith form `[A|a_r|b_r]` has all invariant factors one, so the two images generate all of `K(A)`.

Note the scope distinction: the strict model preserves the threshold-four exchange rule defined at the start of this report, while the relaxed model intentionally changes the sink allocation and **does not** preserve the strict total internal/sink arc counts. The stronger transitivity outcome must not be attributed to the strict valve family.

## 6. A common recurrent configuration and network-dependent sink losses

The state (row-major original labels)

\[
h=\begin{pmatrix}3&1&1\\1&3&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

is recurrent for **all four** networks (`state_code=855`). Add one grain at corner `0`. Exactly one toppling occurs, at the corner, in every network. Nonetheless:

| Graph | Final stable state, row-major heights | Grains lost to sink |
|---|---|---:|
| SP | `(0,3,1,3,3,0,0,0,0)` | **0** |
| SQ | `(0,1,1,1,3,0,0,0,0)` | **4** |
| RP | `(0,2,1,2,3,0,0,0,0)` | **2** |
| RQ | `(0,1,1,1,3,0,0,0,0)` | **4** |

These four outcomes directly follow from the four exact Laplacian columns and pass both the source certificate and independent recurrent membership tests.
