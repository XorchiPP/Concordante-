# Abstract

Let $G=W(F_4)/\{\pm I\}$ be the order-576 central quotient of the Weyl
group of type $F_4$, identified with $V\rtimes(S_3\times S_3)$, where
$V\cong(C_2)^4$ carries the tensor product of two natural
two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-representations. We present a reproducible
computation of $\mathrm H^*(G;\mathbb F_2)$ with eight explicit
homogeneous generators and nine homogeneous defining relations. The
proof combines a Nakaoka collapse for the regular wreath-product Sylow
subgroup, odd-index restriction, an invariant-fiber calculation,
Shapiro’s lemma, and certified Gröbner arithmetic. In particular we
derive an all-degree rational Hilbert–Poincaré series and recover
$\dim\mathrm H^{19}(G;\mathbb F_2)=141$. The manuscript specifies the
finite certificates on which its computational steps depend and
separates the mod-two ring from unresolved integral and secondary
operations. It is offered for independent verification, with
historical-priority assessment reserved for a dedicated literature
review.

**Keywords:** finite group cohomology; Weyl group; $F_4$; invariant
theory; Sylow subgroup; Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence;
Gröbner basis; computer-assisted proof.

20C20.

# 1. Introduction and statement of the result

Cohomology rings of finite groups are natural meeting points of modular
representation theory, group extensions, homological algebra, and
computational commutative algebra. The present calculation concerns the
finite quotient $$
G=W(F_4)/\{\pm I\}\cong V\rtimes Q,\qquad
V=(C_2)^4,\quad Q=S_3\times S_3,$$ where the $Q$-action on $V$ is the
tensor product of its two standard $\mathbb F_2^2$-representations. Thus
$|G|=16\cdot36=576$. The abstract group also occurs as
`SmallGroup(576,8654)` in GAP-compatible catalogs \[5\].

Put $k=\mathbb F_2$. Denote by $x,y,\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_5,w_6,\Theta$
homogeneous classes of respective degrees $$(1,1,2,3,4,5,6,6).$$ Their
construction uses authentic group characters, a central-extension class,
and polynomial characteristic-class restrictions; the polynomial inputs
are specified in the machine-readable accompanying data. In particular,
$\gamma=\mathrm{Sq}^1(\alpha)$.



**Theorem 1** (Explicit graded ring, conditional theorem). *Let
$$A=k[x,y,\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_5,w_6,\Theta]$$ with the indicated
weights, and let $J\subset A$ be generated by the nine homogeneous
relations $R_3,R_{4a},R_{4b},R_{6a},R_{6b},R_{7a},R_{7b},R_{10},R_{12}$
in §7. Subject to the authenticated
computational inputs specified in
§9, the natural evaluation
homomorphism induces an isomorphism of *graded algebras in every
degree*, $$
A/J\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \mathrm H^*(G;k).$$ The Hilbert–Poincaré
series is $$
\boxed{\quad
P_G(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\dim_k\mathrm H^n(G;k)t^n
=\frac{1+2t^2-t^3+t^4-t^8-t^9}
{(1-t)^2(1-t^3)(1+t^2)(1-t^6)}.\quad}$$*



The proof uses all-degree structural arguments. Its infinite-degree
steps use classical theorems of Nakaoka, restriction/transfer, and
Shapiro \[1, 3, 4\]. The computational components are two polynomial
identities on the normal elementary-abelian fiber, authentic evaluations
of nine global relations, and an exact Hilbert-series computation of
their quotient.

#### Contribution and scope.

The result presents $\mathrm H^*(G;k)$ as a graded algebra, with
explicit generators, relations and a rational Hilbert series. The
all-degree integral ring, higher Bocksteins and Massey products form
separate research questions. The Steenrod formulas are documented in a
separate companion note. Historical-priority assessment and external
peer review remain subsequent editorial steps.

# 2. The structural proof architecture



Sylow identification and transfer $\longrightarrow$ additive collapse
and Hilbert series. Fiber invariants and Shapiro $\longrightarrow$
global generation. Authentic relations and exact ideal certificates
$\longrightarrow$ ring isomorphism.



The two independent axes are *dimension* (spectral sequence and Hilbert
series) and *multiplication* (genuine global classes and invariant-fiber
generation). Equality of Hilbert series alone would not establish the
displayed ring isomorphism; the multiplicative filtered-generation
argument is essential.

# 3. Global collapse of the LHS spectral sequence

Consider $$
E_2^{p,q}=\mathrm H^p\!\left(Q;\mathrm H^q(V;k)\right)
=\mathrm H^p\!\left(Q;\operatorname{Sym}^q(V^*)\right)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm H^{p+q}(G;k).$$ Let $P=C_2\times C_2$ be a
Sylow 2-subgroup of $Q$, and $S=V\rtimes P\subset G$; then $[G:S]=9$.



**Lemma 2**. *As a $k[P]$-module, $V$ is the regular permutation module
$k[P]$. Consequently $S\cong C_2\wr P$, the wreath product with
four-element regular $P$-set.*





*Proof.* Each transposition in $S_3\cong\mathrm{GL}_2(k)$ is a
nontrivial unipotent involution. After choosing a suitable basis, it
swaps two basis vectors. Taking tensor products, the two order-two
generators of $P$ act on the four tensor basis vectors by flipping the
first and second indices respectively. The resulting $P$-set is regular.
The underlying additive group of $V$ is therefore the fourfold
$C_2$-base of the regular wreath product. ◻





**Proposition 3** (All-degree additive collapse). *Every differential
$d_r$, $r\ge2$, of the displayed LHS spectral sequence vanishes. Hence its $E_2$ and $E_\infty$ pages coincide as
bigraded vector spaces.*





*Proof.* The Nakaoka wreath-product theorem gives the collapse at $E_2$
for the extension $V\to S\to P$ with coefficients in $k$ \[1, 4\]. For
every $k[Q]$-module $M$ and every $p$, restriction
$\mathrm H^p(Q;M)\to\mathrm H^p(P;M)$ is injective, since restriction
followed by corestriction acts on the source by $[Q:P]=9=1$ in $k$. In
particular, the map of LHS spectral sequences induced by $S\subset G$ is
injective on $E_2$. If a differential for $G$ were first nonzero on some
page $r$, the earlier pages would still have the same $E_2$ terms, so
naturality and injectivity in the target would contradict the
corresponding zero differential for $S$. Thus all differentials vanish.
The assertion is *additive*: a multiplicative identification with $E_2$
does not follow from collapse alone. ◻



# 4. An all-degree rational Poincaré series

Write $K=C_3\times C_3\trianglelefteq Q$. The quotient $Q/K$ is
$P=C_2^2$, and the normal subgroup $K$ has order invertible in $k$.
Therefore
$$\mathrm H^p(Q;\operatorname{Sym}^q V^*)\cong\mathrm H^p(P;(\operatorname{Sym}^qV^*)^K).$$
Extending scalars to $\mathbb F_4$ diagonalizes $K$ without changing
dimensions. Let $a,b,c,d$ denote variables with the four $K$-characters
$(1,1),(1,-1),(-1,1),(-1,-1)$. A monomial $a^A b^B c^C d^D$ is
$K$-invariant if and only if $$
A\equiv D\pmod3,\qquad B\equiv C\pmod3.$$ The residual $P$ acts by the
commuting swaps $(a,b,c,d)\mapsto(c,d,a,b)$ and
$(a,b,c,d)\mapsto(b,a,d,c)$. Let $u_q,v_q,w_q$ count the orbits of
lengths $1,2,4$, respectively, among the invariant monomials of degree
$q$.



**Lemma 4** (Orbit contributions). *For $p\ge0$,
$$\dim_k E_2^{p,q}=\begin{cases}
 u_q+v_q+w_q,&p=0,\\
 (p+1)u_q+v_q,&p>0.
\end{cases}$$*





*Proof.* The orbit permutation module of length $j=1,2,4$ is induced
from the trivial module of a stabilizer of respective order $4,2,1$. By
Shapiro, its cohomology is that of $C_2^2,C_2,1$: dimensions $p+1,1,0$
in positive degree, and dimension one in degree zero. Descend from
$\mathbb F_4$ to $k$. ◻



The generating function for pairs of nonnegative exponents congruent
modulo three is $$F(t)=\frac{1+t^2+t^4}{(1-t^3)^2}.$$ Denote the total
invariant-monomial series by $M(t)=F(t)^2$, and set $C(t)=(1-t^4)^{-1}$.
Burnside counting over the *ordinary integers* gives $$\begin{aligned}
A(t)&:=\sum_{q\ge0}(u_q+v_q+w_q)t^q
=\frac{M(t)+2F(t^2)+(1-t^2)^{-2}}4,\\
B(t)&:=\sum_{q\ge0}v_qt^q
=\frac{2F(t^2)+(1-t^2)^{-2}-3C(t)}2,\\
\sum_{q\ge0}u_qt^q&=C(t).
\end{aligned}$$ The fractions here count integral orbit averages; they
are not operations of dividing by two or four inside $\mathbb F_2$. By
Lemma 4 and Proposition
3,
$$P_G(t)=A(t)+\frac{t}{1-t}B(t)+\left(\frac1{(1-t)^2}-1\right)C(t).$$
Integer rational-function simplification yields exactly the displayed
Hilbert series.

|------:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| $b_n$ |   1 |   2 |   4 |   6 |   8 |  12 |  17 |  21 |  27 |  33 |  39 |

These are the dimensions of $\mathrm H^n(G;k)$, not merely
spectral-sequence upper bounds. In fact, the series has an all-degree
period-12 quasipolynomial formula: for $n=12m+r$, $0\le r<12$,
$$
 b_{12m+r}=a_r+c_rm+(122+4r)\binom m2+48\binom m3,$$ where
$$\begin{aligned}
(a_0,\dots,a_{11})&=(1,2,4,6,8,12,17,21,27,33,39,48),\\
(c_0,\dots,c_{11})&=(56,63,73,82,90,101,111,120,132,143,153,166).
\end{aligned}$$ The all-degree identity, rather than finite
interpolation, follows because the rational denominator divides
$(1-t^{12})^4$; the initial polynomial coefficients determine the four
nonzero finite-difference layers.

# 5. The ring of normal-fiber invariants

The crucial multiplicative input is a presentation of the invariant ring
$k[V^*]^Q$. Consider the genuine restricted classes
$\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_5,w_6,\Theta$. Their full polynomial supports in
four variables $t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3$ are provided in `f4_algebra_data.json`.
Write $$C=k[\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_6]$$ and define $$
B=\frac{k[\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_5,w_6,\Theta]}
{(R_{10}^{V},R_{12}^{V})},$$ where $$\begin{aligned}
R_{10}^{V}={}&w_5^2+\alpha\gamma w_5+\gamma^2w_4+\alpha^2w_6+\alpha^5,\\
R_{12}^{V}={}&\Theta^2+(w_6+\gamma^2+\alpha^3)\Theta\notag\\
&+\gamma w_4w_5+\alpha\gamma^2w_4
+\alpha^2w_4^2+\alpha^3\gamma^2.
\end{aligned}$$ The data check these are literal zero polynomial
identities after substitution of the recorded four-variable supports.



**Proposition 5** (Explicit invariant fiber). *The natural map
$B\to k[V^*]^Q$ induced by restriction is an isomorphism. The $C$-module
$B$ is free of rank four on $1,w_5,\Theta,w_5\Theta$.*





*Proof.* The two equations are monic in $w_5^2$ and, successively,
$\Theta^2$, which supplies the stated free basis. In particular,
$$
\operatorname{Hilb}_B(t)=\frac{1+t^5+t^6+t^{11}}
{(1-t^2)(1-t^3)(1-t^4)(1-t^6)}.$$ For completeness we describe the
injectivity certificate. Over $\mathrm{Frac}(C)$, abbreviate
$a=\alpha,g=\gamma,u=w_4,w=w_6,v=w_5,h=\Theta$, and set
$$\begin{aligned}
A_0&=g^2u+a^2w+a^5,&B_0&=w+g^2+a^3,\\
C_0&=gu,&D_0&=ag^2u+a^2u^2+a^3g^2.
\end{aligned}$$ The two equations become $v^2+agv+A_0=0$ and
$h^2+B_0h+C_0v+D_0=0$. Eliminating $v$ gives the monic quartic
$$\mathcal F(T)=(T^2+B_0T+D_0)^2+agC_0(T^2+B_0T+D_0)+A_0C_0^2.$$ At
$a=g=u=w=1$, this specializes to $T^4+T+1$, irreducible over
$\mathbb F_2$. Monicity and Gauss’s lemma give irreducibility over
$\mathrm{Frac}(C)$; freeness then shows $B$ is a domain.

The four explicit fiber polynomials for $\alpha,\gamma,w_4,w_6$, ordered
by the weighted monomial order with weights $(1,3,3,4)$, have leading
exponent rows
$$\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1&0\\1&0&0&2\\0&0&0&4\\0&2&0&4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=-8\ne0\ \text{in }\mathbb Z.$$ Their leading monomials are
therefore multiplicatively independent, proving algebraic independence
of those four elements. It is *nonzero integer determinant*, not nonzero
determinant modulo two, that is used. The induced prime kernel
$B\to k[V^*]^Q$ must be zero: a nonzero prime ideal of the
four-dimensional domain $B$ would decrease transcendence degree, whereas
the image contains four algebraically independent invariants.

Finally, the Burnside invariant-ring series $A(t)$ from
§4 equals the displayed fiber Hilbert
series as a rational function. Injectivity and equality of each graded
dimension give surjectivity. ◻



# 6. From Shapiro to global generators

A subtle point is that collapse of the displayed LHS spectral sequence
as a *vector-space* spectral sequence does not automatically identify
cup products. We avoid assuming a multiplicative splitting.



**Lemma 6** (Module generation on the $E_2$-page). *The bigraded
$E_2$-algebra is generated by its two edge algebras
$$E_2^{0,*}=k[V^*]^Q\quad\text{and}\quad E_2^{*,0}=\mathrm H^*(Q;k)=k[x,y].$$*





*Proof.* Over $\mathbb F_4$, $K$-invariant monomials form a permutation
basis for $P$. For any orbit $\Omega$, let $H\le P$ be the stabilizer of
a point. Shapiro identifies the contribution to $E_2$ with
$\mathrm H^*(H;\mathbb F_4)$, and the invariant orbit sum maps to its
unit. Multiplication by an inflated base class corresponds to
$\operatorname{res}_H^P:\mathrm H^*(P;\mathbb F_4)\to\mathrm H^*(H;\mathbb F_4)$.
This map is surjective for $H=P,C_2,1$. Hence every orbit summand is a
module over the base ring generated in filtration degree zero. Direct
sums and faithfully flat descent from $\mathbb F_4$ to $k$ establish the
statement. ◻





**Lemma 7** (Finite filtered lifting). *Let $R$ be a connected,
nonnegatively graded algebra with a finite decreasing multiplicative
filtration in each degree. If the initial symbols of homogeneous classes
$z_1,\dots,z_m$ generate $\operatorname{gr}_F R$, then the $z_i$
generate $R$.*





*Proof.* Given a homogeneous $r\in F^pR$, choose a polynomial $h(z_i)$
whose initial symbol agrees with that of $r$. Subtracting raises the
filtration index. Repeating terminates because the filtration is finite
in the chosen total degree. The resulting sum of polynomials equals
$r$. ◻





**Proposition 8**. *The evaluation homomorphism $A\to\mathrm H^*(G;k)$
is surjective in every degree.*





*Proof.* Proposition 5 identifies the six restricted global
classes as generators of $E_2^{0,*}$, and the group characters $x,y$
generate $E_2^{*,0}$. Lemma
6 thus generates all of $E_2$ from the
eight initial symbols. Proposition
3 identifies $E_\infty$ with $E_2$ as
associated-graded terms. Multiplicativity of the LHS filtration and
Lemma 7 finish the argument. ◻

**Proposition 9** (Algebraic exactness and conditional cohomological realization). *The quotient $A/J$ has the displayed Hilbert series in every degree, as verified by the polynomial computations below. Under the genuine-class and detector hypotheses, the nine polynomials evaluate to zero in the actual group cohomology ring.*





*Verification protocol.* The conditional cohomological claim is witnessed by restrictions of
the eight genuine group classes to six maximal elementary-abelian
detector subgroups. The restriction map is injective in each total
degree $\le12$, because the evaluated polynomial generator spans
saturate the independent spectral upper bound there; each defining
relation has degree $\le12$. Thus vanishing of its detector vector is
genuine vanishing in cohomology.

For the algebraic Hilbert-series claim, let $K$ be the ideal generated by the 46 exported polynomials. The accompanying GF(2) verifier proves that they form a Gröbner basis of $K$: 809 noncoprime $S$-pairs reduce to zero and 226 coprime pairs satisfy the product criterion. Reduction of the nine original relations to zero proves $J\subseteq K$. The explicit membership certificate in [ideal-membership](ideal-membership/README.md) supplies 46 identities $g_i=\sum_{j=1}^{9}h_{ij}f_j$, where each $h_{ij}$ is the explicit polynomial obtained by summing the listed monomial multipliers for relation $j$, verified by independent expansion over $\mathbb F_2$. Thus every exported generator lies in $J$, proving $K\subseteq J$ and hence $J=K$. A separate integer least-common-multiple lattice calculation proves $\operatorname{Hilb}_{A/K}(t)=P_G(t)$ by cross multiplication in $\mathbb Z[t]$. Consequently $\operatorname{Hilb}_{A/J}(t)=P_G(t)$ in every degree. This algebraic conclusion uses the frozen polynomial inputs; the cohomological vanishing claim retains the genuine-class and detector hypotheses. ◻





*Proof of Theorem 1.* Proposition
9 makes $\phi:A/J\to\mathrm H^*(G;k)$
well defined. Proposition 8 makes $\phi$ surjective in every
degree. Proposition 9, combined with
§4, establishes equality of the finite
dimensions of each graded component of its source and target. Therefore
every $\phi_n$ is also injective. ◻



# 8. Corollaries and mathematical boundaries



**Corollary 10**. *The 2-modular Betti sequence is fixed by the
displayed quasipolynomial formula for every $n\ge0$, and in particular
$$\dim\mathrm H^{16}(G;k)=98,\quad
\dim\mathrm H^{17}(G;k)=113,\quad
\dim\mathrm H^{18}(G;k)=128,\quad
\dim\mathrm H^{19}(G;k)=141.$$*





**Corollary 11**. *The Krull dimension of $\mathrm H^*(G;k)$ is four,
and the ring is not Cohen–Macaulay.*





*Proof.* The rational series $P_G(t)$ has a pole of order four at $t=1$: its numerator has value three there and its denominator has a zero of order four. The Hilbert–Serre theorem therefore gives Krull dimension four. The displayed Hilbert series behaves as $-t^{-4}$ at infinity. If this dimension-four ring
were Cohen–Macaulay, any homogeneous system of parameters of positive
degrees $d_1,\dots,d_4$ would be a regular sequence; the polynomial
$P_G(t)\prod_{i=1}^4(1-t^{d_i})$ would have nonnegative coefficients.
Its leading coefficient, however, would be negative. This is
impossible. ◻





*Remark 12*. The integral program concerns a separate coefficient
system. Selected additive integral groups and the three-local
presentation have been documented in a subsequent research stage; see
the versioned companion status note. The complete integral cup-product
ring, further central-extension pages and higher Massey products remain

example where invariant theory, Sylow transfer and finite polynomial
