1. Introduction

The H4 root system and the 600-cell support a large family of highly symmetric finite configurations. Most familiar descriptions emphasize vertices, root lines, incidence, distances or Coxeter orbits. Here we focus instead on a relational generation problem.

Start with the 60 projective H4 root lines. A chosen root line p has an orthogonal H3 subsystem. Inside that H3, choose an unoriented order-5 axis a. The ordered pair

  F=(p,a)

will be called a golden flag.

There are 360 such flags. The full Coxeter group W(H4) acts transitively. Two natural golden relations on the H4 root lines encode each flag as an unordered pair of projective roots. These two encodings lead to two adjacency matrices on the 360 flags.

The principal question is:

How much of the full orbital algebra of the W(H4)-action is generated by these two natural relations?

The generated and full algebras have vector-space dimensions 16 and 32. This ratio is not an information-theoretic or experimental percentage. The obstruction is more informative than the dimension count. The obstruction sits in a specific M_2(R) block that both natural generators see only as scalars. That fact forces a minimal completion number of two.

2. The 360-point flag action

Let X_4 be the set of 60 projective H4 root lines.

Fix p=[v] in X_4. The projective roots orthogonal to p form an H3 subsystem with 15 projective root lines. The corresponding icosahedral H3 geometry has six unoriented order-5 axes.

Define

  Omega = {(p,a) : p in X_4, a an unoriented order-5 axis of H3(p)}.

Therefore

  |Omega| = 60*6 = 360.                    (2.1)

The Coxeter group W(H4), of order 14400, acts transitively on Omega. Hence the stabilizer of a flag has order

  14400/360 = 40.                          (2.2)

After quotienting the central antipodal symmetry, the projective group of order 7200 has stabilizer order 20.

PROPOSITION 2.1.

Omega is a transitive W(H4)-set of degree 360 with point stabilizer of order 40.

This is the permutation representation studied throughout the paper.

2.1. Identification with a catalogued reflection-subgroup action

Let s_p denote the H4 reflection whose normal is the projective root line p. In the orthogonal H3 subsystem H3(p), let R_a be the order-ten dihedral reflection subgroup I_2(5) fixing the fivefold axis a pointwise. Define R(p,a)=<s_p> × R_a. The two factors commute and R(p,a) is a reflection subgroup of type A_1 × I_2(5), of order 20.

PROPOSITION 2.2 — Equivariant reflection-subgroup model.

The map (p,a) ↦ R(p,a) identifies Omega equivariantly with the 360-member conjugacy class of reflection subgroups of type I_2(5)A_1 tabulated by Douglass, Pfeiffer and Röhrle [DPR, Table 9].

Proof. The dihedral group I_2(5) has trivial center because 5 is odd. Consequently the only nonidentity central element of R(p,a) is s_p, which determines the root line p. The factor R_a is then recovered as R(p,a)∩W(H3(p)); its one-dimensional fixed space in p-perpendicular determines the unoriented axis a. Thus the map is injective. It is equivariant because reflections, orthogonal subsystems and pointwise stabilizers are preserved under conjugation by W(H4). Table 9 of [DPR] lists exactly 360 reflection subgroups of this type. Since |Omega|=360, the equivariant injection is a bijection. ∎

In particular Omega is the coset action W(H4)/N_W(R), with |R|=20 and |N_W(R)|=40. This catalogued carrier is prior art, not a discovery claimed here. The subject of the present computation is the pair of natural golden generators and their exact relation-algebra completion.

2.2. Classical 600-cell edge-pair realization

The regular 600-cell has 720 edges; Choi and Lee [CL, §3] identify the full W(H4) stabilizer of an (unoriented) edge e as a reflection group of type I_2(5) × A_1 and order 20. Let z=-I_4 denote the central antipodal map. Since the midpoint of e is nonzero, z does not stabilize e. Nevertheless, centrality gives Stab_W(-e)=Stab_W(e)=R_e.

There are 360 antipodal pairs {e,-e}. For each such pair, its stabilizer contains <R_e,z>, of order 40. The regular polytope symmetry action is transitive on edges and hence on antipodal edge pairs, so orbit-stabilizer forces the pair stabilizer to have order 14400/360=40. By the single conjugacy class and normalizer cardinality in [DPR, Table 9], N_W(R_e) also has order 40. Thus Stab_W({e,-e})=<R_e,z>=N_W(R_e), and the assignment {e,-e} ↦ R_e is W-equivariant and bijective onto the class of 360 reflection subgroups. Proposition 2.2 therefore identifies our 360-point flag action with the antipodal-edge-pair action. This is a consequence deduced from [DPR] and [CL], not a novelty claim or a theorem quoted verbatim from either article.

Caution: this equivariant identification of the carrier does not, by itself, identify each of the two golden-shell adjacency matrices with the ordinary line graph of the 600-cell. The two golden charts and their generated algebra remain distinct objects requiring the explicit definitions of §§3–5.

3. Golden relation charts

The geometry around a projective root line p has two golden reflection shells.

Let y be a root in one such shell and y'=s_p(y) its reflection in the hyperplane orthogonal to p. For a suitable unit direction d representing the internal order-5 axis a, one may write

  y  = c_sigma p + r_sigma d,

  y' = -c_sigma p + r_sigma d.

Hence

  [y-y']=[p],

  [y+y']=[a].                              (3.1)

The two shell radii satisfy

  r_-^2 = (5-sqrt(5))/8,

  r_+^2 = (5+sqrt(5))/8.                  (3.2)

Let Gold_+ and Gold_- be the two corresponding sets of unordered projective-root pairs. Each contains exactly 360 pairs.

For sigma in {+,-}, define

  E_sigma : Omega -> Gold_sigma

by sending a flag to its reflection pair in shell sigma.

PROPOSITION 3.1.

Each E_sigma is W(H4)-equivariant and bijective. Thus

  Omega ≅ Gold_+ ≅ Gold_-

as W(H4)-sets.

The same 360-point object therefore has two distinct golden edge charts.

4. Orbital structure

Fix a flag F. Exact orbit enumeration of its stabilizer on Omega gives 32 suborbits, including the diagonal. The subdegree multiset is

  1^1, 2^2, 5^3, 10^18, 20^8.            (4.1)

Hence the permutation action has orbital rank 32.

Let B denote the real orbital algebra, equivalently the real centralizer algebra of the permutation representation.

PROPOSITION 4.1.

  dim_R B = 32.                            (4.2)

The full real Wedderburn type reported in the working manuscript is not certified by the verifier accompanying this edition. This report uses only the dimensions and the specific split ideal certified below.

5. Two natural symmetric generators

Using either golden chart, define a graph on Omega by declaring two flags adjacent when their corresponding golden pairs share one projective H4 endpoint.

Let L_+ and L_- be the two 0-1 adjacency matrices.

Each graph is 22-regular. The nontrivial neighborhoods of a flag in the two graphs are disjoint, so the matrices are not redundant.

Let

  A = alg_R(I,L_+,L_-),                    (5.1)

the unital associative real algebra generated by the two matrices.

Exact closure under multiplication gives

  dim_R A = 16,                            (5.2)

The complete real Wedderburn type of A is likewise outside this verifier’s scope. A is a proper semisimple subalgebra of B. Semisimplicity follows because these finite-dimensional real matrix algebras are closed under transpose: the generators of A are symmetric, while B is the commutant of a finite orthogonal permutation representation.

The commutator

  C=[L_+,L_-]

is skew-symmetric, has rank 68, and satisfies the exact polynomial identity

  C(C^2+1620 I)(C^2+2560 I)=0.            (5.4)

Consequently its nonzero eigenvalues are

  ±18 sqrt(5) i,

  ±16 sqrt(10) i,                          (5.5)

with the multiplicities recorded by the exact certificate.

The certificate verifies the stated commutator polynomial and multiplicities. A classification of every noncommutative simple sector is not claimed by this bounded report.

6. The missing matrix component

The difference between A and B is not a nilpotent radical: both algebras are semisimple.

The exact verifier isolates a central four-dimensional ideal D with one-dimensional center. As B is semisimple, this is a central simple real algebra. A nonzero traceless element satisfies x²=21e_D, ruling out the quaternion division algebra. Consequently there is a simple component D such that

  D ≅ M_2(R)

and the restrictions of the two natural generators are

  L_+|_D = -2 I_2,

  L_-|_D = -2 I_2.                         (6.1)

Thus the image of A in D is only

  R I_2.                                   (6.2)

In this precise sense, D is invisible to the noncommutative structure generated by the two symmetric golden relations.

7. A general one-generator obstruction

The key minimality argument is independent of H4.

LEMMA 7.1.

Let B be a finite-dimensional real semisimple algebra and let D≅M_2(R) be a simple component. Let A subset B be a subalgebra whose image in D is R I_2. Then for every single element T in B,

  alg_R(A,T) != B.

Proof.

Let pi_D:B->D denote the projection to the simple component D. Since pi_D(A)=R I_2, every word in pi_D(A) and T_D reduces to a polynomial in T_D; conversely every polynomial in T_D lies in the projected generated algebra. Hence, exactly,

  pi_D(alg_R(A,T)) = R[T_D].

By the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, every polynomial in the 2x2 matrix T_D reduces to degree at most one. Therefore

  dim_R R[T_D] <=2.

Moreover R[T_D] is commutative, while dim_R M_2(R)=4 and M_2(R) is noncommutative. Hence the projected generated algebra cannot equal D, so the full generated algebra cannot equal B. ∎

Applying Lemma 7.1 to (6.1) gives:

THEOREM 7.2 — One-element completion no-go.

No single additional element T of B—not merely no single orbital matrix—can enlarge A to the full orbital algebra B.

This statement concerns generation by associative multiplication and linear combination over R. It does not claim minimality under a different operation set.

8. Exact two-matrix completion

In the deterministic enumeration of the accompanying verifier, let D_forward be the adjacency matrix of orbital 24 and D_backward the adjacency matrix of orbital 26. The program checks the transpose correspondence, so

  D_backward = D_forward^T.

A second completing pair has labels (25,29). These labels refer only to this enumeration. The endpoint-type pattern described by an earlier implementation is not checked by this verifier and is not part of the certified identification in this edition.

Exact algebra closure gives

  alg_R(I,L_+,L_-,D_forward,D_backward)

  = B.                                     (8.1)

Combining (8.1) with Theorem 7.2 yields the main result.

THEOREM 8.1 — Minimal completion.

For the 360-point H4 flag action, define

  c_B(A)=min{s : there exist T_1,...,T_s in B with alg_R(A,T_1,...,T_s)=B}.

Then

  c_B(A)=2.

The lower bound is Theorem 7.2 and applies to arbitrary elements of B, not only orbital matrices. The upper bound is realized by the two orbital matrices D_forward and D_backward. Consequently the minimum is also two if one restricts the added generators to real orbital matrices. Under a *-closed convention, one of these directed orbital relations together with its adjoint is sufficient and minimal.

Convention on adjoining operators. The number c_B(A)=2 counts added real matrices as individual generators of an ordinary associative algebra. If the admissible closure is instead a real *-algebra and a directed orbital T is automatically adjoined together with T^T, one additional directed orbital family is sufficient, giving c_B^*(A)=1 in that different convention. The result should not be advertised as two physically distinct observations or as a universal lower bound for arbitrary measurements.
